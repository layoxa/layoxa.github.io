Dental acquisition record review · 9 min

Beyond the dental chart audit: build a post-close Care Ledger.

Diligence, migration validation, and record reconciliation answer different questions. An acquirer needs all three—without turning software into a clinician.

August 31, 2026Leyoxa

A dental acquisition creates a precise handoff problem. The buyer inherits years of chart notes, periodontal charting, treatment-plan history, procedure records, and attachments written by clinicians it may never have met. Moving those records into a destination system does not, by itself, identify which documented sequences still need review or who owns the next step.

Three reviews, three different questions

A diligence chart audit asks whether documentation supports the transaction and whether clinical, billing, or compliance risks require attention. Dental-association acquisition resources explicitly include patient-chart review in buyer due diligence and ask buyers to examine how recommended treatment appears in the records.

A migration validation asks whether authorized records, demographics, documents, images, and links arrived in the destination environment with the expected counts and mappings.

A post-close acquisition record review asks a third question: across the approved source history, what documented care state lacks a later reconciling record, and who should review it now?

This is not a claim that treatment mining or multi-location due-diligence analytics are new. Dental Intelligence publicly markets DSO analytics and due-diligence support, while Jarvis Analytics describes data mining and opportunity analysis. Leyoxa's narrower distinction is the transaction-specific evidence package and buyer handoff.

Important boundary

A missing later record is not proof that care was untreated, necessary, or appropriate. The permitted output is a record state—for example: evidence documented; diagnosis not recorded; dentist should re-evaluate.

What the Care Ledger contains

The Care Ledger is a source-linked portfolio artifact, not a diagnosis engine. Each item should carry its originating location and system, source date, record type, reconciliation state, recorded plan value where present, accountable owner, next review action, and explicit limitations.

  • Clinical review: the smallest high-priority slice that requires a dentist.
  • Existing visit: reviewed items that can be attached to an already scheduled encounter.
  • Coordination: documented, previously diagnosed care that an authorized team may re-present.
  • Source verification: incomplete or ambiguous records held until evidence is resolved.

Start with gates, not a connector list

Before full production access, an acquisition review should prove three things. First, every source has a permitted read path. Second, a sample shows enough chart-note quality and field depth to support the agreed output. Third, counsel and the buyer approve the reporting language, retention, responsibilities, and escalation protocol.

Open Dental, Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Curve, and other PMS names describe acquisition environments—not automatic integration claims. Access may require approved exports, vendor cooperation, database-level methods, or a qualified third party. Unsupported fields and unavailable history belong in the deliverable.

Some vendors publish integration surfaces—Open Dental publishes an API specification and CareStack maintains a developer portal—but technical availability does not replace authorization, vendor terms, security review, or appropriate agreements. When a service provider handles protected health information for a covered entity, the parties must assess the applicable HIPAA business-associate requirements and safeguards.

Keep ledger value separate from revenue

A treatment-plan amount recorded in a source system is context, not expected collections. Any commercial model should keep recorded plan value, reviewer-approved amount, scheduled work, completed production, and collected revenue separate. Practice-specific assumptions can be shown as ranges, but should never be presented as guaranteed recovery.

Where the operating layer begins

The acquisition engagement ends with an exportable ledger, methodology, limitations, worklist, and audit trail. With separate authorization after close, the same provenance and ownership model could support a mixed-PMS operating brief or bounded agents. That is product direction. The available offer today is the fixed-fee, read-only Acquisition Review.

A dated transaction creates the operating deadline

Recent announcements show the scale of the handoff: Park Dental announced an agreement involving 12 locations in August 2026; Dentalcorp announced its acquisition of a 21-practice Florida platform in July 2026; and Sage Dental announced a nine-practice Tampa Bay acquisition. Each transaction has its own systems, obligations, and integration plan—the reason a reusable but source-specific evidence package matters.
