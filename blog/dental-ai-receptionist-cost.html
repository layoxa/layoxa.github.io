Dental AI receptionist cost:
what you actually pay for.

Monthly plans are only one line item. PMS integration, phone usage, onboarding, compliance, and exception handling determine the real cost.

Leyoxa/Blog/AI Receptionist Cost

The cost of a dental AI receptionist cannot be judged from the monthly headline alone. A low-cost answering bot may take a message. A production dental voice agent must understand appointment types, check provider and operatory availability, write safely into the practice management system, transfer exceptions, protect patient information, and give staff a usable record of every call.

For buyers, the better question is: what does it cost per correctly booked and attended appointment?

How dental AI receptionist pricing works

Most vendors combine some of the following components:

  • Platform fee: a monthly or annual subscription per practice location
  • Usage: per-minute voice, phone number, SMS, recording, or transcription charges
  • Onboarding: practice rules, call flows, voice calibration, phone routing, and staff training
  • PMS integration: connection to Dentrix, Open Dental, Curve, Eaglesoft, or another system
  • Customization: multi-location routing, specialty workflows, languages, or unusual scheduling logic
  • Support: monitoring, call review, prompt changes, and escalation tuning after launch

The five costs hidden behind the monthly price

1. Phone and model usage

Voice calls consume telephony, speech recognition, language-model, and voice-synthesis services. Ask whether usage is included, metered, or subject to a fair-use limit. Model a busy month, not the average shown in a sales deck.

2. Practice management integration

Message-taking is inexpensive. Reliable booking is not. The system must resolve patients, read capacity, apply duration and provider rules, prevent duplicates, and reconcile changes. Confirm whether the advertised plan includes real PMS writes or only a request sent to staff.

3. Implementation time

Someone must document insurance answers, appointment types, transfer rules, emergency language, office hours, and the situations the agent must not handle. A rushed setup lowers the invoice and increases corrections later.

4. Compliance and security

If calls contain protected health information, ask for a BAA, encryption details, role-based access, retention controls, subprocessor documentation, and audit logs. A cheaper non-compliant workflow is not a comparable product.

5. Human exception handling

No agent should automate every conversation. Practices still need a clear destination for clinical questions, distressed callers, unsupported insurance cases, payment disputes, and unusual scheduling requests.

A current pricing example

As of August 2, 2026, Praxis lists all six of its connected dental agents at $497 per location per month on a monthly plan, plus $997 onboarding. Its annual plan is listed at $397 per location per month with onboarding waived. Pricing can change, so confirm the live page before making a purchasing decision.

That bundle is broader than a voice-only receptionist: it also includes GEO / SEO, Smile, Executive Assistant, Reputation, and Call Coaching. When comparing quotes, normalize the included workflows instead of comparing two monthly numbers with different scope.

How to calculate break-even

Use completed appointments, not calls answered. A simple model is:

Monthly value = additional attended appointments × average contribution per appointment + staff hours returned × loaded hourly cost.

Then subtract subscription, usage, onboarding amortized across the expected term, and internal oversight. Keep new-patient bookings, recall appointments, and reschedules separate because their economics differ.

Questions to ask every vendor

  • Does the agent create appointments directly in our PMS?
  • Which setup, usage, support, SMS, and phone costs are not in the headline?
  • Will you sign a BAA, and which subprocessors receive call data?
  • How are double bookings, outages, retries, and staff edits handled?
  • Can we review call outcomes and restrict access by staff role?
  • What happens when a caller asks something the agent cannot safely answer?
  • How do you measure booked, attended, and completed appointments?

When the cheapest plan costs more

A low monthly fee loses its advantage if staff must re-enter every request, correct bad bookings, or review unstructured transcripts. The best dental AI receptionist is the least expensive system that safely closes the full workflow for your practice—not the smallest number on a pricing page.
