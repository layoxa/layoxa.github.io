What happens to the patient history after the second, fifth, and tenth acquisition?

The problem is not only that locations use different systems. Each acquisition adds another decade of narrative that has never been reconciled longitudinally.

Leyoxa Care Ledger is a product direction pending acquisition-cohort proof. Every PMS requires a permitted access path and sufficient record depth before scope is accepted.

After the second practice: local memory still works

Leaders can still ask the treatment coordinator why a case was not completed or which note explains a deferral. The risk is that the answer lives with a person rather than in a repeatable review.

After the fifth: histories stop being comparable

Different charting habits, treatment-plan conventions, and benefit records make a group-level report look consistent while the source meaning differs by location. A count of inactive plans cannot explain whether evidence was documented, care was declined, or a sequence stalled.

After the tenth: unreconciled care becomes structural

Manual chart review cannot scale across ten histories. The group needs source provenance, stable reconciliation states, location boundaries, and a dentist-owned review process before any follow-up occurs.

A safer acquisition sequence

  1. Preserve the acquired PMS as the source of truth.
  2. Approve a read-only scope and fixed historical window.
  3. Map evidence, narrative, treatment-plan history, and time context.
  4. Return source-linked ledger items for local dentist review.
  5. Measure reviewer agreement before expanding to another location.

Do not pretend every PMS reads the same

Access method, field depth, note structure, and historical quality differ. A vendor name is not proof of a usable reconciliation. Each deployment should state what can be read, which dates are included, and which records are unavailable.

The acquisition does not need another system imposed on day one. It needs a disciplined way to learn what the existing record already contains.
