Agents + human approval · 8 min

Where AI agents belong in dental operations.

After the data is connected and the recommendation is explainable—not before.

An agent is useful when it can perform a bounded task inside a reliable operating context. Giving an agent broad access to disconnected systems does not create intelligence; it creates more places for silent failure.

Start with six states

  1. Observe. Read only the fields required for the approved purpose.
  2. Detect. Identify an anomaly or opportunity using visible rules and source evidence.
  3. Recommend. Propose a next step, owner, value range, and confidence.
  4. Approve. Apply the practice's human-approval policy.
  5. Act. Execute through a supported connector with idempotency and a failure path.
  6. Verify. Confirm the operational or financial result in the authoritative system.

A realistic chair-fill workflow

The schedule exposes an opening. Recall and treatment records identify an approved cohort. A coordinator reviews the criteria. An outreach agent sends or places the approved communication. The PMS records the booking. The morning brief later distinguishes contacted, responded, booked, completed, and collected states.

Human language should configure, not bypass control

An operator may prompt: “For hygiene openings in the next 72 hours, build a queue of eligible overdue patients, require coordinator approval, stop after two attempts, and report booked production by location.” AI can assemble the workflow from approved components. It should not invent unavailable connectors, expand the patient cohort, or remove approval because a prompt asked it to.

Agents need ownership and escalation

Every workflow needs an accountable business owner, system owner, permitted purpose, allowed actions, approval threshold, quiet hours, retry limit, fallback recipient, audit log, and kill switch. If an insurance call encounters ambiguous coverage, it should route to staff. If a patient asks a clinical question, it should stop and hand off.

Clinical boundaries remain clinical

AI may summarize source-linked operational context or identify that a required record is missing. It should not diagnose, alter a chart, or present treatment without a clinician's decision and the practice's approved communication protocol.

The result belongs in the brief

Agents should not live in a separate automation universe. Their accepted work, failures, escalations, and verified outcomes belong beside the underlying opportunity. That gives the operator one place to see what the system observed, what a person approved, what the agent did, and what changed.

The practical future is not autonomous dentistry. It is faster, more consistent dental operations with explicit human authority.
