Live · Praxis Executive Assistant · 5 min read

One brain for
every system
your clinic runs.

How the completed Praxis Executive Assistant connects clinical, financial, phone, payroll, and marketing data—then turns fragmented signals into operational answers.

Leyoxa / Blog / Praxis Executive Assistant

Most clinics do not have a data shortage. They have a connection problem. The practice management system knows what happened in the schedule. The phone system knows which calls converted. QuickBooks knows what was collected. Payroll knows what labor cost. Google and Meta know which campaigns generated demand. Each system tells the truth about one slice of the business—and none explains the whole.

That is the problem we set out to solve with DDS Marketing. The project that began as Office Brain is now complete and available inside Praxis as the Executive Assistant: one conversational operational layer across the systems a dental practice already uses.

Connect the systems, not another dashboard

Traditional practice reporting asks an owner to open several dashboards, export spreadsheets, reconcile definitions, and interpret the result. The Executive Assistant reverses that workflow. It connects to the source systems and lets the operator ask a question in plain English.

  • PMS: production, appointments, providers, no-shows, recalls, and treatment plans from systems such as Dentrix, Open Dental, and Curve
  • Phones: call volume, missed opportunities, booking outcomes, and conversations that require staff follow-up
  • Finance and payroll: collections, expenses, lab bills, and staffing costs from QuickBooks, Gusto, and connected accounting tools
  • Marketing: campaigns, spend, leads, reviews, and attribution from GoHighLevel, Google, Meta, and the practice's web presence

The result is not a new source of truth competing with the PMS or ledger. It is a governed layer that joins their signals, preserves where each number came from, and makes the relationship between them queryable.

From “what happened?” to “why?”

A production report can show that the week finished below target. An operational brain can explain that three no-shows created most of the gap, hygiene recall conversion also fell, and 18 due patients were never confirmed. That answer is useful because it connects the financial outcome to the scheduling cause.

Operators can ask questions such as:

  • Why did production drop this week?
  • Which campaign actually booked implant cases?
  • What is our true cost per new patient after media spend and labor?
  • Which location has open chair capacity but enough unworked recall demand?
  • How much did we spend on lab bills last quarter?

Because the answer crosses clinical, financial, and acquisition data, a question that once required an office manager, bookkeeper, and marketing agency can be investigated from one place.

Optimization means closing the loop

Visibility is only half the job. Praxis agents share context, so an approved next step can move from analysis into execution. If the assistant detects open chair time and an overdue recall list, Voice can work the list. If calls are reaching the office but not converting, Call Coaching can identify the repeated breakdown. If discovery is weak, GEO / SEO and Reputation can improve the sources patients and AI search engines use to evaluate the practice.

This creates a simple operating loop:

  1. Observe: connect live signals from every relevant system
  2. Explain: relate outcomes to their likely operational causes
  3. Recommend: propose the highest-leverage next action
  4. Approve and act: keep the operator in control while the appropriate Praxis agent executes
  5. Measure: read the resulting booking, production, reputation, or conversion data back into the same loop

One source of operational context across locations

The multi-location problem is even sharper. Different offices may use different PMS configurations, phone routing, campaign structures, and bookkeeping categories. Praxis normalizes those sources so an operator can compare locations without flattening away the details that explain the differences.

That makes the Executive Assistant useful at two altitudes: a practice manager can investigate tomorrow's schedule, while a group owner can ask which location is underperforming and why.

Healthcare data still needs boundaries

Connecting more systems increases the importance of access control and auditability. Praxis runs on HIPAA-compliant infrastructure, signs a BAA by default, and encrypts call recordings, transcripts, and patient data in transit and at rest. Operational answers should also be scoped by role: a marketing user does not need payroll access, and a location manager does not automatically need group-wide finance data.

The goal is not to pool everything carelessly. It is to make authorized data useful without losing its provenance, permissions, or audit trail.

The Brain is done. The operating loop is live.

Office Brain was our working name for a missing layer in dental software. Praxis Executive Assistant is the completed product: one brain that reads the systems a practice already runs, answers hard operating questions in plain English, and can coordinate the agents that improve the result.
