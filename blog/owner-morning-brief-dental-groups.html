Operating intelligence · 8 min

The owner morning brief for a dental group.

Not another dashboard. One ranked list of where attention and money can move today.

A growing dental group rarely has one clean operating system. One office may use Dentrix, another Open Dental, a newly acquired practice Eaglesoft, and a de novo location Curve. Phones, payments, marketing, insurance, accounting, and payroll add more systems. The owner's problem is not access to reports. It is deciding what deserves attention first.

Rank locations by exception, not by dashboard tab

A useful morning brief should compare the signals that affect provider-hour yield: production, chair utilization, unanswered calls, recall, unscheduled treatment, collections, reviews, and marketing. It should elevate anomalies rather than force an operator to inspect eight reports for every location.

Each item needs a location, time window, source evidence, accountable owner, urgency, confidence, estimated financial range, and recommended next step. Without those fields, an alert is only more information.

One opportunity object across mixed systems

Consider an office with six open hygiene hours tomorrow. The schedule identifies the capacity. Recall data identifies patients who may be appropriate to contact. Phone and message systems record whether outreach occurred. The PMS confirms whether an appointment was booked. The ledger later shows whether production occurred.

No single system owns that entire chain. The operating layer should preserve each source while creating one opportunity object above them.

Money changes the priority

“Recall is below target” is not enough. A group should see an estimated opportunity range and how it was calculated. For example: six available provider-hours, a location-specific production-per-hour range, an eligible recall cohort, and a booking probability based on the practice's own history. The range should be visible and editable; it is an estimate, not guaranteed revenue.

The brief must end in accountable work

The top item may be assigned to a hygiene coordinator, attached to tomorrow's huddle, or offered to an approved outreach agent. Lower-confidence items may remain in review. Clinical recommendations stay with clinicians. The software should never hide which person or agent owns the next step.

Close the loop

The most defensible operating metric is a progression: opportunity identified, work accepted, outcome verified. A booked appointment is different from scheduled production, and scheduled production is different from collected cash. The brief should report each state separately and maintain the attribution window.

That is how a morning brief becomes more than conversational reporting. It gives a heterogeneous group one operating rhythm without forcing every location to replace the systems it already uses.
