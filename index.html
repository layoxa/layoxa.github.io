What this looks like in a dental practice

A connected system should solve a specific operating problem—not add another dashboard.

01 · DemandA new patient calls after hours

The voice workflow answers approved questions and identifies intent.

02 · ContextRules and availability are checked

Provider, appointment type, operatory, location, and scheduling constraints are applied.

03 · ActionThe appointment is booked or escalated

A valid slot is written to the supported practice system; exceptions reach staff with context.

04 · LearningThe outcome returns to operations

Source, booking result, attendance, and follow-up can be measured together.

Market sequence

Built for North American dentistry.

US practices are the immediate focus. Canada follows with the same operating model adapted to federal, provincial, vendor, and clinic requirements.

Now · United States

Deploy around US dental workflows

Start with a bounded operational use case, document responsibility boundaries, execute required vendor agreements, and configure access, retention, audit, and escalation controls for the actual stack.

Next · Canada

Adapt for Canadian practices

Reuse proven workflow and integration patterns while addressing PIPEDA, applicable provincial privacy rules, data handling, consent, and the systems used by each practice.

Later · Additional markets

Expand after the model holds

New regions come after repeatable US and Canadian delivery—not before. Local requirements are treated as implementation inputs, not marketing footnotes.
