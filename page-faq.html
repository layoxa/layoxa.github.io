FAQ

Direct answers.

What Leyoxa reads, what it returns, what it never decides, and how the first proof works.

01

What can I buy now?

A fixed-fee Dental Acquisition Record Review. Leyoxa tests access and source quality, reconciles an approved read-only snapshot, and returns a source-linked Care Ledger, owner-assigned worklist, methodology, limitations, and audit trail.

02

What records are reconciled?

The approved scope may include free-text chart notes, periodontal charting, treatment-plan history, procedure records, and benefit-period context.

03

Does Leyoxa diagnose?

No. A permitted output is: evidence documented; diagnosis not recorded; dentist should re-evaluate. The practice records clinical decisions in its PMS.

04

Does Leyoxa write to the PMS?

The Acquisition Review and Care Ledger are read-only. A separate operational workflow may write or communicate only when the connector supports it and the purpose, approval, audit, and failure path have been configured.

05

Which PMSs can be reviewed?

The acquisition cohort is sampled before scope is accepted. Open Dental, Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Curve, and other systems require a proven, permitted read path and sufficient field depth; a vendor name alone is not a capability claim.

06

What is the commercial offer?

A fixed acquisition and integration engagement priced by location count, PMS mix, historical depth, access path, close date, and delivery window. The acquirer receives readiness findings, a source-linked portfolio ledger, owner-assigned handoff, methodology, limitations, and audit trail.

07

Is the software “HIPAA compliant”?

Compliance is a shared operating responsibility, not a blanket label. Controls, BAA requirements, subprocessors, access, retention, audit, and each party’s responsibilities are defined for the engagement.

08

What is product direction rather than available now?

After close, the same provenance and ownership model could support AI-built dashboards, work queues, opportunity views, and governed agents. Those interfaces are product direction—not part of the current review engagement—and would never override source permissions or autonomously make clinical decisions.
